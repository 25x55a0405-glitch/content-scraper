# Win agencies first, then chase accountants

The best first product is a **"client report agent" for small marketing and SEO agencies, UK first and then US**. It is a workflow that pulls each client's analytics and ad data, drafts the monthly commentary, checks every number in the draft against the source data in a loop, and waits for an account manager to approve the report before anything goes out. Sell it as a **fixed-price pilot of about $900 covering three of the agency's clients**. Find buyers by sending personalised Loom videos to agency owners and bidding on Upwork. Build it in n8n (a visual workflow tool) on the agency's own accounts. It does not win because it has the biggest market. It wins because it is the only candidate that does well on every test at once: low liability, data that is not sensitive, buyers who already outsource and pay retainers, work that can be done asynchronously across the India time difference, and short runs that n8n handles without custom code. **Document chasing for small UK accounting practices has the strongest demand evidence** (about 7 in 10 firms name chasing clients for missing documents as the first task they would hand to an AI agent) and longer-lasting clients. That makes it the right second offer, once you have a case study. It is not the right first one, because a chase loop waits days for humans (where no-code tools strain), the data is sensitive, and the buyers are cautious. Skip receptionists, lead generation and support bots. Zoom now sells an AI receptionist for $29.99 a month, and the median Upwork AI-automation budget is $150. One caveat runs through everything below. Almost all pricing figures come from agency and vendor marketing blogs or search-result snippets, and the notes found **no independently verified case study of a solo builder** in any vertical. Treat every number here as a rough direction, not a benchmark.

## The money sits above a $150 median, in exception-heavy back-office work

The freelance market for "AI automation" is big but split in two. Upwatcher counted **3,324 Upwork AI-automation posts in May 2026, about 100 a day, with a median fixed budget of $150, and more than half of fixed-price posts under $25** ([Upwatcher](https://www.upwatcher.io/market/ai-automation/)). The same keyword covers "$100 'set up an agent' gigs and $50,000 multi-tenant outreach engines." US buyers dominate: one June 2026 sample found **998 US AI postings against 177 from the UK** ([Vibeworker](https://tryvibeworker.com/blog/upwork-job-market-report-june-2026)). The paid demand for real multi-step agent graphs sits in the top slice of this market and in direct agency deals, where builds run $3,000–$15,000 and retainers $1,500–$5,000 a month ([Lets-Viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026-what-buyers-pay); agency blog). It is not in the median post. The category is still growing quickly: Upwork reported that its **AI Integration & Automation spending grew more than 90% year on year in Q4 2025** ([Upwork](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-financial)).

Which kinds of work actually pay? A one-week sample of 542 Upwork agent jobs put **back-office "paperwork" automation first at 15.2% and customer support second at 14.8%** ([Greenice](https://greenice.net/ai-agent-development-trends/); August 2025 data). Customer support is a trap for custom builders, because **over 90% of enterprises are trying third-party support tools rather than building their own** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-enterprise-2025/)). The agents companies keep in production are also far from fully autonomous. A study of 306 practitioners found that **68% of production agents run 10 or fewer steps before a human steps in, 74% rely mainly on human review, and 66% are allowed to take minutes or longer to respond** ([arXiv 2512.04123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04123v1)). That describes agent graphs in plain terms: fixed steps, branches, retries and a human approval gate. It also points to the best jobs for them: paperwork that branches on exceptions, loops until it is complete, and needs a sign-off. Gartner expects **over 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by end-2027** because of cost, unclear value or weak risk controls ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robertszczerba/2026/07/07/why-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-may-be-canceled-by-2027/)). So buyers will want proof of return on investment and visible safety controls before they commit.

This also explains why the receptionist attempt failed, and the reason is not just bad luck. It got three things wrong at once. The product was a commodity: Zoom launched a standalone AI receptionist on **July 9, 2026 at $29.99 a month**, on top of a crowded white-label reseller scene that sources describe as "race-to-the-bottom" pricing ([Voksha](https://voksha.com/guide/why-tech-giants-ai-receptionists-2026/); vendor blog). The buyer was wrong too. Micro-businesses buy $30 software subscriptions, while firms with **50–249 employees show the highest AI adoption, at 77.9%** ([Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-back-office-automation-statistics-2026)). The channel was the third mistake. Calling local businesses live, from another time zone, as an unknown seller, takes on the hardest possible trust problem. Any new offer has to change all three, not just the product.

## Five offers survive; two clear the bar for a first client

The research covered twelve verticals. Seven drop out quickly. Receptionists, basic chatbots and lead generation are commoditised. Law, immigration and mortgage or financial advice carry regulatory liability that a non-coder should not take on: the UK's SRA holds the solicitor accountable for any AI output ([VinciWorks](https://vinciworks.com/blog/is-sras-ai-warning-a-wake-up-call-for-uk-law-firms/)), and **giving UK immigration advice without registration is a criminal offence** ([LexisNexis UK](https://www.lexisnexis.com/en-gb/legal/guidance/giving-immigration-advice-in-the-uk)). In property management, software is rising fast and the work is local and carries habitability risk. Creators are reachable but low-budget and tend to do it themselves. Startups' security-questionnaire work is capped by $10,000–$20,000-a-year software and by in-house engineering ([Wolfia](https://wolfia.com/blog/conveyor-reviews-pricing-alternatives)). Recruiting survives only for admin and onboarding. Candidate screening triggers rules like **NYC Local Law 144's mandatory bias audits** ([Metaview](https://www.metaview.ai/resources/blog/ai-hiring-laws-2026-2027)), and the ATS market is very crowded. The five concrete offers that remain are defined below.

| Rank | Offer (vertical + workflow) | Entry offer | Main channel | Stack |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agency Report Agent**: UK/US marketing and SEO agencies (5–50 staff). Pull GA4, Search Console and ad data, draft commentary, a number-check loop, a branch that flags anomalies, account-manager approval in Slack, then send | $900 fixed pilot on 3 of the agency's clients, 14 days, money back if drafts are not usable | Personalised Loom videos to agency owners, plus Upwork agency posts | n8n on agency-owned accounts; Claude/OpenAI on the agency's API key; Google Sheets as the tracker |
| 2 | **Practice Chase Agent**: small UK accountancy practices (Ltd/LLP). Request list, client upload, classify and extract, completeness check, re-chase loop, preparer sign-off (year-end and MTD quarterly data) | Paid pilot on 10 of the practice's clients for one filing cycle | Loom and email to Ltd/LLP practices; Upwork back-office posts | n8n plus a client-owned tracker sheet, sitting on top of their practice software (Karbon/TaxDome) |
| 3 | **White-label build partner** for US/UK automation and marketing agencies | Wholesale hourly or per-project work behind the agency's brand | Upwork subcontracting posts, n8n forum, the same agency-owner list | Whatever the agency uses (usually n8n/Make) |
| 4 | **Renewal Prep Agent**: US independent insurance agencies (about 10 staff). Pull the expiring policy, re-collect exposure data, compare quotes, producer review; certificate-of-insurance requests | Pilot on one renewal month | State associations and Big "I" communities, Loom | n8n plus browser automation for carrier portals |
| 5 | **Broker Inbox Agent**: small US freight brokers on legacy TMS. Classify carrier and shipper email, extract lane and rate, draft quote, broker approval, enter into TMS | Pilot on one inbox | LinkedIn, r/FreightBrokers, Loom | n8n plus TMS API or spreadsheet |

**Agency Report Agent.** Demand evidence is medium and comes from case studies. A report agent that pulls GA, social and ad accounts reportedly **replaced 3 hours per client per month**, with a human review step kept in before sending ([GetDevDone](https://getdevdone.com/blog/ai-powered-client-reporting-for-marketing-agency.html); vendor case). A 14-person agency claims it saved about **80 hours a week** by moving reporting, research and content prep to agents ([Burrak AI](https://burrakai.com/blog/marketing-agency-ai-automation-case-study); vendor claim). Agencies already outsource. White-label partners sell embedded build capacity **from $1,499 a month** ([White Label IQ](https://www.whitelabeliq.com/white-label-ai-services/)), and a Shopify agency posted on Upwork for an "AI Workflow Developer for [a] Shopify Agency Operating System" ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Workflow-Developer-for-Shopify-Agency-Operating-System_~022069781483899170925/)). The pressure on agencies helps and hurts at once. **36–38% of UK agencies say clients have taken work in-house because of AI** ([Mode Marketing](https://modemarketing.ai/uk-agency-market-2026/)), which gives them a reason to cut delivery costs but also tightens budgets. Reporting dashboards such as Whatagraph already exist ([Whatagraph](https://whatagraph.com/blog/articles/ai-marketing-workflows)), so the custom value lies in the agency-voice commentary, the cross-source number checks and the approval flow, not in charts.

**Practice Chase Agent.** This offer has the strongest single demand data point in the research. **Nearly 7 in 10 accounting firms picked chasing clients for missing documents as the #1 task they would hand to an autonomous agent** ([Financial Cents](https://financial-cents.com/resources/guides/the-state-of-ai-in-bookkeeping-accounting/); search snippet from a vendor survey). In the UK, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax began in April 2026 for sole traders and landlords earning over £50,000. Before launch, **42% of accountants were not prepared and only 10% "very prepared"** ([IRIS](https://www.iris.co.uk/news/m-t-d-readiness-survey/); vendor survey). Because MTD replaces one annual collection with quarterly updates, the chase loop now runs four times a year, which makes the pain recurring rather than seasonal. The price anchor is concrete: an offshore Philippine bookkeeper costs about **$1,800–$2,200 a month** ([Helpware](https://helpware.com/blog/accounting-outsourcing-companies-philippines)). Competition is the weak point. Karbon, Financial Cents, Liscio and others all market document collection already ([Liscio](https://www.liscio.me/blog-posts/how-accounting-firms-can-automate-client-document-collection); [Assembly](https://assembly.com/blog/karbon-pricing)), so a custom build has to orchestrate around them rather than replace them.

**White-label partner.** This is a channel more than a product, and its economics are clear. Agencies pay offshore builders **"often $15 to $35 per hour" and charge clients $100–$250** ([Boundev](https://www.boundev.ai/blog/white-label-ai-teams-agency-margins)). Indian n8n juniors list at $10–$25 an hour ([Ciphernutz](https://ciphernutz.com/blog/hire-n8n-expert-cost-pricing-guide)). Subcontracting posts appear openly on Upwork ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/freelance-jobs/apply/Development-Subcontracting-Partner-Build-What-You-Sell-White-Label-Partnership-Opportunity_~022060463966798843596/)). You avoid sales and client-facing liability. In exchange you get low rates and usually an NDA that blocks a public case study.

**Renewal Prep Agent.** The intent signals are strong. **Two-thirds of US independent agencies plan to increase AI use, but only 8% have it embedded in daily workflows** ([IndependentAgent.com](https://www.independentagent.com/news/two-thirds-of-independent-agents-plan-to-increase-ai-use-this-year/)). The market is about **37,000 agencies averaging 9.9 staff** ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/09/24/886639.htm)). The problems are that renewals run through carrier portals and quote-only management systems like Applied Epic, which means brittle browser automation. Agencies are also licensed and name data privacy as their top AI concern (24%), and every working hour falls overnight in India.

**Broker Inbox Agent.** This offer has the most pain and the clearest return. Vendors claim quote response times fell from about 45 minutes to under 5 and win rates rose from 18% to 27% ([GetTransport](https://blog.gettransport.com/trends-in-logistic/ai-agents-freight-brokers-2026-quote-automation/); vendor claims). But a freight-specific platform, **Debales, has reached $1M ARR bootstrapped** with integrations to six major TMSs (same source). **Broker margins fell to 16.9% in July 2026** ([Fleet Equipment](https://www.fleetequipmentmag.com/freight-broker-carrier-survey-2026/)), and FreightWaves describes 2026 as a "perfect storm" of brokerage failures ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/?p=567368)). That combination brings payment risk and a fast-closing window.

## Head-to-head contests decide the order

Rating offers one by one hides the trade-offs. The ten matchups between the five offers make them visible, and a few cross-category contests settle the remaining choices.

| Matchup | Winner for a first client | Deciding factor |
|---|---|---|
| Agency Report vs Practice Chase | **Agency Report** | Chase has better demand evidence and stickier clients, but it involves sensitive financial data, cautious buyers and loops that wait for days. Reports finish in minutes and only read marketing metrics |
| Agency Report vs White-label | **Agency Report** (run white-label in parallel) | Retail price and a case study you can show, versus wholesale $15–35/hr under NDA. You reach both through the same list of agency owners |
| Agency Report vs Renewal Prep | **Agency Report** | Reach, liability and buildability. Carrier portals and Applied Epic are hard for a non-coder, and US hours fall overnight in India |
| Agency Report vs Broker Inbox | **Agency Report** | Freight pain is higher, but quoting is real-time, purpose-built software is arriving fast, and broker margins are collapsing |
| Practice Chase vs White-label | Chase for building a business; white-label for quick cash | Chase builds an asset you can turn into a product. White-label pays this month |
| Practice Chase vs Renewal Prep | **Practice Chase** | UK time-zone overlap, lower liability and fewer system-access hurdles outweigh insurance's stronger stated intent (2/3 vs 8%) |
| Practice Chase vs Broker Inbox | **Practice Chase** | Asynchronous loop versus real-time quoting |
| White-label vs Renewal Prep | **White-label** | Faster, with no sales cycle |
| White-label vs Broker Inbox | **White-label** | Same reason |
| Renewal Prep vs Broker Inbox | **Renewal Prep** | Renewals run on cycles of weeks or months. Quotes have to go out in minutes |

The vertical-against-vertical contests in the notes point the same way. Agencies beat creators, with similar reach but bigger budgets and a retainer habit, while creators can copy free repurposing templates for themselves ([GitHub](https://github.com/TrueSkillMaster/content-repurposing-pipeline)). Freight beats property management on pain, but **AppFolio's Realm-X with its 50-unit floor** ([AI Pro Playbook](https://aiproplaybook.com/tools/appfolio-realm-x)) shows how quickly vertical software is filling the property space. Accounting beats law and immigration almost entirely on liability. The deeper pattern is that the most saturated functions are single-turn or linear tasks that software can template. The least saturated involve company-specific systems, exception loops and approval chains, which is exactly where agent graphs earn their keep. In every vertical, "AI adoption" still mostly means chat assistants and meeting notes. **Bullhorn finds only 10% of staffing firms run agentic AI across the full workflow** ([Bullhorn](https://www.bullhorn.com/news-and-press/press-releases/bullhorn-grid-report-staffing-firms-using-ai-see-stronger-growth-faster-placements/)), and Kitces finds that **integration, not AI, is the biggest driver of advisers' tech satisfaction** ([Digital Alpha on Kitces](https://www.digital-alpha.com/integration-before-ai-what-the-2025-kitces-research-really-says/)). The gap is in orchestration across the systems a firm already owns.

Channel against channel has a clear answer for speed. Upwork is the fastest and cheapest way to a first dollar. Proposals cost about $0.30–$3.75 in Connects at **$0.15 each**, with a 0–15% fee ([Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/upwork-connects-explained-2026); [Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/blog/upwork-fees-freelancers)). **Micro jobs under $500 win 10–18% of the time, against 3–7% overall** ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/benchmark-overview-reply-shortlist-win-rates-by-category-budget)). Upwork's weaknesses are small first deals and an AI/ML category that GigRadar calls a "saturated trap," with replies below the platform mean ([GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-job-categories-agencies)). Loom outreach beats plain cold email by a wide margin: plain cold email averages **3.43% replies** ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/?lng=en)), while personalised videos reportedly reach **10–16%** ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/video-cold-email); vendor and anecdote data). Videos only work when they are specific to the prospect, though. Vetted networks are closed for now: **Toptal accepts about 3%** ([EarnifyHub](https://www.earnifyhub.com/freelancing-gig-work/toptal-vetting-process-review-2026)), Fiverr Pro wants experience with large businesses, the Zapier Experts directory requires case studies ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/l/new-experts/info)), and n8n's partner programme is a closed pilot. Each of these becomes available after the first one to three paid projects, not before. On LinkedIn, do manual, low-volume outreach only. The platform's terms ban scraping and high-frequency automation ([Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/linkedin-automation-2026)).

Offer against stack is where the brief's "LangGraph-style" wish meets practical limits. The research found that LangGraph is justified "when the agent itself is the product or the critical path, particularly when explicit state, cycles, checkpoints, replay, streaming, and human approval are central" ([Cipher Projects](https://cipherprojects.com/blog/posts/n8n-agents-vs-custom-agent-frameworks/)). n8n 2.x already offers loops, branches, error workflows, built-in evaluations and an official feature where **a specific tool call pauses until a person approves it in Slack, Teams or email** ([n8n docs](https://docs.n8n.io/advanced-ai/human-in-the-loop-tools)). It is free to self-host. For someone who cannot read code, being able to see and debug every run visually decides the matter. The security evidence backs this up. **AI-written code contained vulnerabilities in 45% of Veracode's test tasks** ([Veracode](https://www.veracode.com/press-release/ai-generated-code-poses-major-security-risks-in-nearly-half-of-all-development-tasks-veracode-research-reveals/)), and in another study **79.3% of agent-written solutions that passed functional tests were still insecure** ([arXiv 2512.03262](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.03262v2)). Multi-agent "crews" are a weaker default than one agent in a constrained graph, because measured failure rates ran **41%–86.7%** across leading multi-agent frameworks ([MAST, arXiv 2503.13657](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.13657v2)). Buyers rarely ask for a framework by name. n8n alone appears in **357 Upwork AI postings** ([Upwatcher](https://www.upwatcher.io/market/ai-automation/)), while no reliable count of LangGraph requests was found. On the pricing side, hybrid models win: **95% of 80 AI-agent companies use hybrid pricing and only 3.8% pure outcome pricing** ([Orb](https://www.withorb.com/blog/ai-agent-pricing-trends)). For this builder, a setup fee plus a monthly care plan beats both hourly billing, which punishes AI-speed building, and pay-per-outcome, which loads quality risk onto the person who can't debug the code.

## Seven angles that only appear when the notes are combined

**Asynchronous approval steps turn the India time difference into an advantage.** The production study shows that most paying agents tolerate minutes of latency and have a human review step ([arXiv 2512.04123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04123v1)). A monthly report or a document-chase cycle does not need the builder awake during client hours. Freight quoting does: its whole value is cutting response time from roughly 45 minutes to under 5 during US business hours. The timing of the work, not just the size of the market, rules out real-time operations for someone working from India. IST is 4.5–5.5 hours ahead of the UK, so UK clients overlap with Indian evenings, while the US East Coast overlaps only with late night.

**How long the workflow waits decides the stack, and the stack decides the first vertical.** The stack research says n8n strains when "state must persist across long waits." A document-chase loop waits days for each client to upload files. That is exactly the case that pushes a builder toward LangGraph or a durable-execution engine, meaning code they cannot read. A report run finishes in minutes. For a non-coder, this is a hidden reason the agency offer should come before the accounting one. When the chase offer does come, a practical workaround (my inference, not tested in the sources) is to keep state outside the workflow. Each client gets a row in a client-owned tracker sheet, and a scheduled n8n run reads and updates it. The workflow then never has to "remember" anything across days.

**One outreach list opens two doors.** The go-to-market note found that agency owners can judge a technical demo and that agencies post subcontracting work. The commerce note found that agencies resell AI services to their own clients at **$300–$1,500 a month** ([Vendasta](https://www.vendasta.com/blog/white-label-ai-agents/)). The same Loom to an agency owner can therefore carry two offers: "I'll automate your monthly reporting," and "if you sell AI to your clients, I can build it behind your brand." The first offer earns a case study at retail price. The second fills spare capacity.

**The chase loop is a product that works across verticals.** The professional-services note found the same pain in all six verticals it studied: chase missing documents or information, check what comes back, re-chase, then get a human sign-off. That covers accounting year-end, legal intake, insurance renewals, recruiting onboarding, mortgage fact-finds and immigration evidence bundles. The commerce note found the same loop in supplier purchase-order chasing and carrier check calls. A builder who perfects one chase graph can re-skin it for each vertical. That is the "agent-as-a-service" model the stack research calls the highest-leverage tier, at $300–$2,500 a month per client ([Pickaxe](https://pickaxe.co/post/how-to-start-ai-agent-agency); vendor blog).

**An evaluation report counters the buyer's biggest fear.** Gartner's cancellation drivers are cost, unclear value and weak risk controls. LangChain's 2026 survey shows that **89% of teams monitor their agents but only 52% run evaluations**, and that quality is now the top barrier to production ([nocode.tech summary](https://www.nocode.tech/article/langchain-report-quality-not-cost-killing-ai-agents)). A builder who gives the client a one-page test report differentiates on the exact thing buyers worry about. The report shows 20 past reports re-run, every number checked, the pass rate, and hours saved. It also justifies the monthly fee.

**UK email law quietly reshapes the target list.** Under PECR, cold email to limited companies and LLPs is allowed with an opt-out, but **sole traders and simple partnerships count as individuals and need consent** ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/rules-emailing-corporates-consumers-marketing-promotions/)). In a vertical full of very small practices, that removes part of the accounting list before outreach even starts. Agencies are more often limited companies (my inference; not quantified in the notes). In the US, CAN-SPAM allows cold B2B email but carries penalties of **up to $53,088 per non-compliant email** ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/can-spam-penalties)).

**The verification skill carries over to the second vertical.** The core technical skill in the report agent is checking AI-written text against source numbers in a loop. That is the same skill an accounting practice needs for categorising transactions with low-confidence items flagged for human review, and for MTD quarterly data gathering. The order of the two offers is therefore not arbitrary. Agencies teach you the verification loop and produce a case study. Accountants then pay for the same discipline, at retainer values that vendor sources put at **$1,500–$3,000 a month with lifetimes of 18+ months** for professional services ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/start-ai-automation-business-case-studies); vendor claim).

## The pick: a self-checking report agent for agencies

The recommendation is the **Agency Report Agent**, sold to UK marketing and SEO agencies with 5–50 staff, then to US agencies once you have a case study. The workflow is a true agent graph, not a chatbot. It fetches each client's data with retries when an API fails. It branches by which channels each client uses. It drafts commentary in the agency's voice. A verification loop, capped at three attempts, compares every figure in the draft to the source and rewrites any mismatch. An anomaly branch flags big swings for a human to explain. Finally, an approval step in Slack lets the account manager approve, edit or reject before anything reaches the client. The agent only reads marketing metrics and never sends anything on its own. That keeps the damage small if something goes wrong in code the builder cannot read, which is the risk the security evidence most warns about.

The pick wins on combined fit, not on any single number. Its demand evidence is weaker than accounting's and its price ceiling lower than freight's. It is still the only offer that scores 4 or 5 on liability, buildability, reach from India and time to first dollar together. It also avoids all three of the receptionist attempt's failure modes: it is not a commodity product, the buyer has 5–50 staff, and the channel is asynchronous and demo-led. Your personal assistant, "Stars," belongs in the first ten seconds of every Loom as proof that you ship. The Loom itself has to show the report agent working on a mock-up for that particular agency's kind of client, because generic videos get skipped.

Several things should change this recommendation. If **100 personalised Looms to agencies produce fewer than about five replies**, the agency market is not responding to this pitch. Switch the same outreach to UK accounting practices with the chase demo. If you have **a warm introduction to a UK accounting practice** (a relative, a lecturer, a family accountant), start with the Practice Chase Agent, because its demand evidence is stronger and a warm contact removes the trust barrier that ranks it second. If **a white-label agency offers steady work at $25+ an hour**, take it as income but keep building the agency offer under your own name, since white-label rarely produces a case study you can show. If agencies say the reporting software they already use has added good AI summaries, the custom value has gone. Move to cross-tool workflows such as SEO content pipelines with a QA step, or to accounting. Finally, if a verified source contradicts the Financial Cents "7 in 10" finding, accounting drops from second place.

## Thirty days to a first invoice

The path below assumes about 3–4 focused hours a day around classes. The 30-day target is ambitious. With roughly 150 Looms at a 10–16% reply rate, the arithmetic gives 15–24 replies and perhaps a handful of calls. That rests on vendor reply-rate data and assumes a close rate nobody has measured, so plan for 45–60 days and treat 30 as the best case.

| Days | Goal | Concrete actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 | Build the demo and the storefront | Build the report agent in n8n on dummy or your own site data: fetch, validate, retry, draft, number-check loop (max 3), anomaly flag, Slack approval, PDF. Write a 20-case test set and a one-page eval sheet. Record a 3-minute Loom. Set up Upwork (with a Loom offer and guarantee in proposals) and a Contra portfolio. Build your own n8n Upwork job-alert workflow so you can bid within minutes |
| 8–14 | Fill the pipeline | Build a list of 150 UK agencies (5–50 staff, Ltd/LLP checked on Companies House). Send 10–15 personalised Looms a day with a compliant opt-out and postal address. Bid on 2–3 Upwork reporting or agency-ops jobs a day, aiming for one $150–$500 micro job to earn a first review |
| 15–21 | Convert replies | Hold 20-minute calls. Map their current reporting time per client. Offer the $900 pilot on three clients with a money-back guarantee. Also mention white-label capacity |
| 22–30 | Deliver and upsell | Run the pilot on the agency's accounts. Send the eval sheet showing hours saved per report. Propose the rollout: $2,500 to cover all clients plus a $600–$750/month care plan. Ask for written permission to describe the work anonymously |

The pricing sits deliberately below published agency bands, because the buyer is taking a chance on an unknown overseas student. Agency guides put focused builds at $3,000+ and retainers at $1,500–$5,000 a month ([Lets-Viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026-what-buyers-pay)). They also put standalone paid audits at **$500–$2,000, often waived if the client buys the build** (same source). The $900 pilot works as a paid audit and proof at once. The care plan follows a common rule of thumb of **15–25% of the setup fee per month** ([Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/)). The ROI pitch rests on the vendor-reported 3 hours saved per client per month. For an agency with 20 clients, that is about 60 hours a month. Pass model costs straight through on the agency's own API key with a spend cap. At current list prices of **$1/$5 per million tokens for Haiku 4.5 and $3/$15 for Sonnet 4.6** ([Finout](https://finout.io/blog/anthropic-api-pricing)), a month of reports should cost tens of dollars. Uncapped loops are the main way that number explodes. One vendor-reported incident describes looping agents running up a **$47,000 bill over 11 days** ([dev.to/Waxell](https://dev.to/waxell/the-47000-agent-loop-why-token-budget-alerts-arent-budget-enforcement-389i); unverified).

The guardrails are part of the product, not optional extras. Everything runs on **client-owned accounts**: the agency's n8n workspace or VPS, its API keys and its data connections, with you invited as a user. That limits your liability and makes leaving clean. API access is read-only. No report goes out without a human approving it. Every loop has a hard iteration cap and every key has a provider spend limit, because budget alerts are not budget enforcement. Re-run the test set after any change to a prompt, model or workflow. Keep custom code to a minimum. If any appears, run an AI security review and a secret scanner on it, since prompting a model to "write secure code" did not fix the problem in the SusVibes study. The contract should state the scope, client acceptance testing, and that autonomous correctness is not guaranteed. For payment, use Upwork escrow or, off-platform, Skydo, Wise Business or Payoneer, and keep a FIRA for every receipt. Below **₹20 lakh a year, GST registration is not mandatory** for this zero-rated export of services ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/gst-for-freelancers)). **Stripe India is invite-only** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/stripe-shifts-to-invite-only-model-in-india-plans-infrastructure-boost-by-2025)). Do not form a US LLC yet: a foreign-owned LLC must file Form 5472 every year, with a **$25,000 penalty for not filing** ([Terms.law](https://terms.law/Invest-USA/legal-compliance/form-5472.html)). A CA should confirm the income-tax treatment, which the notes did not research.

## Where the evidence is thin

The overall direction is well supported: back-office loops over commodity front-office bots, mid-sized firms over micro-businesses, human approval as standard practice, and n8n before code. The specific numbers are much weaker. Researchers could not open many primary pages directly, so a large share of figures came from search-result summaries. Most pricing comes from agencies and software vendors who profit from high numbers.

Several gaps matter specifically for this decision. No source measured time-to-first-client for beginners, let alone for Indian students. No source compared free and paid pilots. No source quantified how many agencies actually buy custom reporting automation rather than dashboard software. No source checked how US/UK buyers view Indian student freelancers. Reddit and practitioner forums were mostly unreachable, so first-hand complaints from agency owners and accountants are missing. The pilot itself is the cheapest way to fill most of these gaps.

## Conclusion

Picking a vertical turns out to be less about market size than about how well the workflow's shape matches the builder's limits. A non-coder in India is best served by workflows that wait politely for humans (asynchronous approval), finish quickly (no multi-day state), only read data (a small blast radius), and sell to buyers who already outsource. That combination is rarer than "big market with pain," and it points to agencies before accountants, even though accountants have the better demand data. The receptionist failure was a mismatch of exactly this kind, not proof that selling AI doesn't work.

The longer-term opportunity is not any single vertical. It is the reusable "chase until complete, verify, then ask a human" graph that recurs in almost every paperwork-heavy business, sitting on top of software that firms already pay for but that does not connect their systems. Vertical software is adding native AI quickly, so the window for custom builds may be 12–24 months. Use the first client to learn the verification loop, the second vertical to prove the chase loop, and then turn the pattern into a product before the software companies close the gap.
