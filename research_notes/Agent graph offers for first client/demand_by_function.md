## Q1. What AI agent / AI automation projects are most commonly posted on Upwork, Contra, Toptal and similar in 2026, and at what budgets? Are there published analyses of job-post data?

### Takeaway
Several independent analyses of Upwork job posts exist (Greenice: 542 AI-agent jobs, Aug 2025; Upwatcher: 3,324 "AI automation" jobs, May 2026; Vibeworker: 127,607 jobs, June 2026; GigRadar: 133,872 proposals, Dec 2025–Feb 2026). They agree on two points. Volume is high: roughly 100 AI-automation posts a day, with the US the largest buyer and the UK a distant second. Budgets are sharply split: the median fixed budget is only about $100–150, and most posts are cheap "set up an agent" gigs. A thin top tier of $5K–$50K builds sits above that. Back-office/paperwork automation, customer support and marketing/outreach are the most common use cases.

### Inferences
- The Upwork median AI-automation job (about $100–150 fixed, about $30/hr) is a low-end market. Paid demand for true multi-step agent graphs sits in the **top decile (P90 $60+/hr, $2.5K–$50K fixed)** and in direct/agency channels ($1.5K–$5K/mo retainers, $20K+ builds), not in the median post.
- The US is about 5.6x the UK by Upwork AI-post count (998 vs 177 in Vibeworker's sample). UK demand is real but small on marketplaces.
- Large fixed-price posts are shrinking (-18.8% for >$5K in Q2 2026), and AI/ML is crowded with bidders (below-mean reply rates). A first client is likely easier to win through a niche/vertical framing than a generic "AI agent developer" pitch.

- No marketplace data was found that separates "agent graph" scopes (loops, HITL, retries) from simple single-step automations.

## Q2. Comparable per-function evidence matrix, and which functions show the fastest-growing spend on custom agents vs buying SaaS

### Takeaway
Back-office/finance ops (AP, invoices, reconciliation, document extraction) is the most consistently cited paid custom-agent use case. It is the top Upwork use case (15.2%), carries clear per-unit ROI, and SaaS covers only part of it. Customer support is the largest departmental spend category, but enterprises are overwhelmingly **buying** support SaaS (90%+ testing third-party apps). That makes support a weaker custom-build target except for complex, integration-heavy ticket resolution. Coding dominates departmental spend but is SaaS-captured (Cursor, Claude Code).

### Inferences
- **Highest custom-build fit:** finance ops / AP and insurance intake. Both have clear per-unit ROI, exception loops that SaaS handles poorly, mid-market buyers with real budgets ($15K–$150K builds), and lower SaaS saturation. Back-office is also the most frequent Upwork agent use case.
- **Large spend but "buy" dominant:** customer support and coding. The spend is real, but buyers default to SaaS, so custom work is limited to integration-heavy edge cases.
- **Fast-growing but early:** research/knowledge management and IT ops lead agent *adoption* (McKinsey, LangChain), but the freelance budget evidence is thin and buyers are mostly enterprises, which are harder for a first client.
- Buyers are shifting AI budgets into recurring line items (a16z), which supports a build-plus-retainer model ($1.5K–$5K/mo) over one-off gigs.

### Gaps
- No source separates **custom-build spend from SaaS spend per function** with growth rates. Menlo's build-vs-buy split by function could not be accessed (menlovc.com was blocked). The often-cited "76% purchased vs built" figure was **not verified**.
- No reliable buyer-size distribution per function for Upwork AI-agent jobs was found.
- E-commerce ops, RFP and due-diligence have no quantitative 2025–26 job-post evidence.
- AP ROI figures (e.g., $1.34M–$1.86M annual benefit) come from AP-automation vendors (Peakflo) and are likely optimistic.

## Q3. Which workflows inherently need loops, human approval or multi-step reasoning (where agent graphs are a real advantage)?

### Takeaway
Production evidence shows that the agents companies actually pay for are **constrained, mostly static workflows with human checkpoints**, not free-roaming autonomy. 68% run 10 steps or fewer before human intervention, 74% rely on human-in-the-loop evaluation, and 66% tolerate minute-plus latency. That fits graph frameworks (explicit nodes, retries, interrupt-for-approval) best in exception-heavy, document-heavy, regulated workflows: AP exceptions, claims intake, compliance review, hiring shortlists, and research with verification.

### Cited Findings
- "Measuring Agents in Production" (UC Berkeley/IBM et al., arXiv 2512.04123, ICLR 2026): 306 practitioners across 26 domains plus 20 case studies. **80% deploy for productivity. 70% use off-the-shelf models without tuning, and 79% use manual prompts. 68% execute ≤10 steps before human intervention, 74% rely primarily on human-in-the-loop evaluation, and 66% allow response times of minutes or longer.** Reliability is the top challenge. Domains include finance and insurance. — [arXiv 2512.04123](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04123v1); [IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/publications/measuring-agents-in-production)
- The-Decoder summary: corporate agents "use simple workflows with human oversight instead of chasing full autonomy". — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/corporate-ai-agents-use-simple-workflows-with-human-oversight-instead-of-chasing-full-autonomy/)
- A common production pattern is an LLM judge scoring confidence on each output, auto-accepting high-confidence cases and routing uncertain ones to humans. — [LangChain State of AI Agents (via search summary)](https://langchain.com/stateofaiagents)
- AP agents: extract invoice data, match against POs, route approvals and flag discrepancies. These are explicit branch and approval steps. — [Biz4Group](https://www.biz4group.com/blog/ai-agent-development-cost)
- Insurance pricing separates single-workflow triage ($15K–$30K) from multi-agent underwriting/fraud "with HITL governance and regulatory compliance" ($60K–$85K). Buyers pay a premium for HITL and audit trails. — [QServices](https://www.qservicesit.com/pricing/ai-agent-development-cost-for-insurance-carriers)
- Gartner (June 2025): **over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by end of 2027**, due to escalating costs, unclear business value or inadequate risk controls. — [Forbes, Jul 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robertszczerba/2026/07/07/why-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-may-be-canceled-by-2027/); [BERI](https://www.beri.net/article/40-percent-agentic-ai-projects-canceled-2027-gartner)
- Agency retainers explicitly cover "error monitoring, adjustments when upstream APIs change" (the retry/maintenance burden of multi-step workflows). — [Lets-Viz](https://lets-viz.com/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026-what-buyers-pay)

### Inferences
Workflows where an agent graph is a real advantage over a chatbot or a linear Zap:
- **AP/invoice exceptions:** branching on match/mismatch, a loop to request a corrected invoice, approval thresholds as HITL interrupts, and retries on ERP/API failures.
- **Claims/quote intake:** a loop until the document set is complete, routing by claim type, and adjuster approval before payout or quote.
- **Compliance/document review:** checklist iteration, escalation on ambiguous clauses, and a human sign-off audit trail.
- **Recruiting screening:** batch scoring, then human shortlist approval, then outreach and scheduling with follow-up loops.
- **Research/due diligence:** iterative search, verification and citation, with a reviewer step.
- **Support tickets:** only when actions are taken (refunds, account changes) under approval gates; simple FAQ answering does not need a graph.

Gartner's cancellation drivers (cost, unclear value, risk controls) point to scoping offers around **measurable per-unit ROI plus built-in HITL and audit logging**, which graph frameworks provide natively.

### Gaps
- No marketplace data was found that quantifies a price premium for "LangGraph/agent graph" over "n8n/Zapier" builds for the same function.
- There is no direct evidence of how many US/UK buyers specifically ask for LangGraph (vs n8n, Copilot Studio, CrewAI) in job posts. n8n's count (357 posts) is known; LangGraph's was not found.

## Q4. Which functions are already commoditized (AI receptionists, lead gen, basic chatbots)? What is the evidence of saturation?

### Takeaway
AI voice receptionists, basic chatbots/FAQ bots, and cheap lead-gen/outreach automations show clear commoditization: big-tech entry at about $30/month, white-label reseller platforms, "race-to-the-bottom" pricing, sub-$150 median Upwork budgets, and below-average reply rates in the AI/ML category. Customer support is SaaS-captured at the enterprise level.

### Cited Findings
- **AI receptionists:** Zoom launched a standalone AI receptionist on **July 9, 2026 at $29.99/month for 100 minutes**. A crowded white-label/reseller ecosystem exists (Trillet, Answrr, OnCallClerk, MyVoiceAIConnect). Markup-based reseller models face "commoditized, race-to-the-bottom pricing pressure". ### Inferences
- Saturation ranking (most to least saturated, based on the evidence above): AI receptionists ≈ basic FAQ chatbots > lead gen/outreach ≈ support deflection > recruiting screening > content ops > AP/finance exceptions > compliance/document review > insurance intake > due diligence/research.
- Saturated functions are mostly **single-turn or linear** tasks that SaaS can template. Less saturated functions involve **company-specific systems (ERP, claims platforms), exception loops and approval chains**, which matches where agent graphs add value.

### Gaps

## Q5. What do Gartner, McKinsey, Menlo Ventures, a16z and others report for 2025–2026 enterprise and SMB agent spend by function?

### Takeaway
Enterprise GenAI spend tripled to $37B in 2025 (Menlo) and is growing about 75% YoY (a16z). Departmental spend is coding-heavy (55%), followed by IT ops, marketing and customer success at about $0.6–0.7B each. Agent *scaling* remains low: 10% or less in any function (McKinsey), led by IT and knowledge management. Gartner expects 40%+ of agentic projects to be canceled by 2027. UK AI spend is the fastest-growing in Europe (+151% YoY), but it is mostly subscriptions.

### Cited Findings
- Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025): **$37B enterprise GenAI spend in 2025, up from $11.5B (3.2x)**. Departmental AI was $7.3B: coding $4.2B (55%), IT ops $730M, marketing $660M, customer success $630M. Vertical AI was $3.5B (healthcare-led) and horizontal copilots $8.4B. — [Menlo press release](https://markets.financialcontent.com/dowtheoryletters/article/gnwcq-2025-12-9-menlo-ventures-2025-state-of-generative-ai-report-enterprise-investment-hit-37b-in-2025-tripling-in-one-year); [NoJitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025)
- Note on conflicting framing: one NoJitter headline cites "$19B in GenAI spend during 2025" while Menlo's release says $37B total. The $19B likely refers to the application layer only; the precise split was **not verified**. — [NoJitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025)
- a16z (2025, 100+ CIOs across 15+ industries): AI spend is **+75% YoY**, and the innovation-budget share fell from 25% to 7%. 90%+ are testing third-party support apps. — [a16z](https://a16z.com/ai-enterprise-2025/)
- McKinsey State of AI 2025: **62% experimenting with agents, 23% scaling somewhere, at most 10% scaling in any function**. IT and knowledge management lead. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/22/10-of-enterprise-functions-use-ai-agents-mckinsey-finds/)
- Gartner (June 2025): **40%+ of agentic AI projects canceled by end-2027**. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robertszczerba/2026/07/07/why-40-of-agentic-ai-projects-may-be-canceled-by-2027/)
- LangChain: 57% have agents in production. Top use cases are research/summarization 58%, productivity 53.5% and customer service 45.8%. — [LangChain](https://langchain.com/stateofaiagents)
- SMB: small-business AI adoption was 55–58% in 2025 (up from 39–40% in 2024). **Mid-market (50–249 employees) had the highest adoption at 77.9%**, vs 76% for large enterprises and 66% for SMEs. — [Stealth Agents research](https://stealthagents.com/research/startup-back-office-automation-statistics-2026); [Digital Origin, SMB tech spend 2026](https://www.digital-origin.co.uk/insights/blog/smb-technology-spend-in-2026/)
- UK: AI spend **+151% YoY, the fastest in Europe**. 70% of AI transactions are subscriptions and 72% of UK organizations have adopted AI (Spendesk). — [Spendesk](https://www.spendesk.com/press/ai-state-of-spend-report/)

### Inferences
- Mid-market companies (50–249 employees) combine the highest adoption with budgets for $15K–$60K builds and retainers. They look like the best-fit first-client segment for custom agent graphs, ahead of micro-SMBs (who buy $30/mo SaaS) and large enterprises (who buy platforms or build in-house).
- Finance, HR and Legal are each a small share of departmental spend (3–7%), but they are where SaaS penetration is lowest and workflows are exception- and approval-heavy. That leaves "white space" for outside builders, as opposed to markets that are already big but captured.

### Gaps
- No analyst source gives **agent-specific spend by function** (as opposed to GenAI spend overall) or a **custom-build vs SaaS split by function**.
- No Gartner 2026 figure for agent spend by function was found. The "Gartner 2026 Voice AI Market Report" cited by a vendor is unverified.
- McKinsey's full per-function agent-scaling table (beyond "IT and knowledge management lead") could not be retrieved.
- US-only vs UK-only splits are unavailable in Menlo, a16z and McKinsey; those surveys are global or US-centric.
